# The story bible

Keeping a cast of hundreds straight: who they are, who they know, and which
chapters they turn up in.

Every story has one, from the **Bible** button on the story page. Anyone who
can read the story can read its bible; only the author and coauthors can
change it.

## Writing an entry

**New entry** asks for a name, what kind of thing it is — person, place,
group, thing or event — and a description. Two of the fields are worth more
than they look:

- **Also called.** Ranks, nicknames, maiden names, a ship's pennant number.
  This is what makes the chapter scan honest: a scene that only ever says
  "the Old Man" still counts as an appearance.
- **Spoilers.** What you know and the reader does not. It stays folded away
  on the entry page.

Every name you write down also joins the story's spelling dictionary, so the
editor stops underlining your cast.

## You do not tag appearances

**Appears in** is worked out by reading the current text of every chapter,
looking for the entry's name and its aliases. It runs again whenever a
chapter gains a version, so deleting a name from a scene removes that scene
from the entry.

It only sees names, so it is wrong in two directions: somebody present but
never named is missed, and a name that is also a ship or a common word is
found too often. **Correct this**, on the entry, ticks and unticks chapters
by hand, and your correction outlives every rescan.

One rule worth knowing: **if two entries answer to the same name, it counts
for neither.** Guessing would put people in scenes they are not in. The
index says which names are contested and who is fighting over them — give
one of them a distinguishing alias and the appearances come back.

## Relations

Add one from either end: a word for the relation, who it points at, and
optionally what it is called the other way ("sister of" / "brother of"). It
is stored once and shown on both entries, so the two cannot end up
disagreeing. Linking the same pair again edits that relation instead of
growing a second one.

## Pictures

Several per entry, with captions. The **first one is the entry's face** — at
the top of its page and as a thumbnail in the cast list — so changing the
portrait means moving a picture to the front.

PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP. Large pictures are shrunk in your browser before
they are sent, so you are not waiting on an upload.

## Custom fields

Two kinds, and you can use both.

- **A template per kind**, from *Set the fields every entry of a kind is
  asked for* at the foot of the entry form. Every person gets asked for a
  Rank and a Home world; every ship for a Class. That is what makes a big
  cast comparable instead of a hundred private habits.
- **Extras on one entry**, for what that entry alone needs. The label box
  offers back every label already used in this story, so "Rank" gets reused
  instead of reinvented as "Grade".

Taking a label out of the template never deletes what an entry already said
under it: it stops being asked for and stays where it was answered.

## Starting from a story that is already written

You do not have to type a hundred forms. The bible finds people you have
written down, but it also **finds the names you have not**: open a chapter
and look under the text for *names here are not in the bible*. It is
guesswork over the prose, so some of it will be wrong — the top of the list
is usually your cast, and one click writes the entry. The same panel is in
the chapter editor, working on the draft you have not saved yet.

## While you write

A chapter page lists **who is in it** and marks who is **new here**. Names in
the prose are **links to their entries**, with the one-line summary on hover;
where a name is also a wiki page, your own entry wins. And the editor has a
box for writing somebody down without leaving the chapter.
