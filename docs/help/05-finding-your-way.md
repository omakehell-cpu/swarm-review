# Finding your way round

The three pages you will spend most of your time on -- the front page, a
story, a chapter -- and what each part of them is for.

## The front page

**Stories**, in the top bar, is the front page. It is weighted the way the
group works: **writing** first, then **reviewing**, then **reading** what
is finished.

@figure front-page.png | The front page: Write and Review side by side, then Read.

**Write**, on the left, is your desk: each story of yours still being
written, with where it stands, its latest chapter, how far it is towards
its word target, and **+ New chapter**. A draft you have not published yet
is said there -- *Draft of chapter 4, saved 2 hours ago* -- with
**Continue the draft**. With nothing on the go, it offers to start a story.

**Review**, beside it, is everything that wants your eyes, most personal
first:

- **Asked to read by you** -- a writer asked you to read a chapter, with
  the question they want answered. See
  [asking somebody to read](/help/reading-and-reviewing).
- **Notes on your chapters** -- notes you have not answered yet, by
  chapter.
- **Replies to you** -- somebody answered a note you left, or named you.
- **New in what you follow** -- new chapters in the stories you follow
  (see below), which open the first of them.
- **New in the group** -- other chapters published since you last looked
  that you have not opened, a story at a time. The chapters that came in
  with an imported story are never here.

**Read** is the stories themselves. Above them, **Following** (every story
you follow, and whether there is anything new) and **Recently read** (the
stories by other people you have been reading, with where to pick each
one up, or that you read it to the end). Then three shelves:
**Being written** (where the list opens), **Finished** and **All**, each
with how many it holds. A story its writer has set aside is in All.

The front page shows a dozen of a shelf: enough to see what is there
without a wall of titles. **See all** under them opens the rest in the
advanced search, on the same shelf and in the same order.

Beside the shelves, the order -- **Latest** or **A–Z** -- and the look:

- **Rows**, the catalogue: one story to a row, with its cover, its title,
  who wrote it and its series, its blurb and its tags, and on the right
  the facts to compare it by -- where it stands, chapters, words, how long
  it takes to read, and when it last moved.
- **Table**, for going through a shelf of hundreds: one line each, with
  the same facts in columns.

The look you choose is kept, here and in the advanced search.

Where a story stands is always a mark and a word -- **✓ Finished**, **✎
Being written**, **On hiatus**, **Set aside** -- never a colour alone.

### Searching

The **search box in the top bar** looks through everything: stories,
chapters, the text of every chapter, the wiki and the glossary. The lens
beside it searches; the lens with two sliders in it opens the **advanced
search**, which is for stories, with every way of narrowing them at once:

- **Title, blurb or tag**, and the **author** -- part of a name is enough.
- **Words in the text** -- anywhere in the chapters; put a phrase in
  "quotes".
- **Where it stands**, **length**, **series** and **tags** (a story has to
  carry every tag you pick).
- **Only stories I follow**, and the order, including **only stories I
  write in**.

What is switched on is shown above the results, each with a × to take it
off. Thirty stories to a page, numbered. Every search is its own address,
so it can be bookmarked or sent.

### Following a story

**☆ Follow**, on a story's page -- and at the foot of its latest chapter
-- stars it. From then on, every new chapter somebody else puts up in it
is news for you until you have read it: a number beside **Stories** in the
top bar, the story under **New in what you follow**, and the chapter in
your [feed](/help/your-account), if you use one. Press it again
(**★ Following**) to stop. The number beside the star is how many people
follow the story.

At the foot, **Lately** is the last three things people did -- read a
chapter, left notes, asked for a read -- and **All activity** is the whole
list. It is there so that you can feel other people are around, not to be
read like a feed.

**The first time you come in**, a short welcome takes the place of all
this: read a chapter, leave a note, put up something of your own. Each
step ticks itself off as you do it, and *I know my way round* puts it away
for good.

**When the site has changed** since your last visit, the front page says
so once, in a few lines, the first time you open it. After that the
changes live under **Help**, in [What's new](/help/changelog), and the dot
on *Help* in the top bar tells you when there is something there you have
not seen.

## A story

Click a story's title to get to its page. It opens like the front of a
book: the cover, the title, who wrote it, the blurb, and one button to
start -- **Start reading**, or **Continue with chapter 4** once you are
part-way through, or **Read it again from the start** when you have read
it all.

@figure story-page.png | A story's page: the title page, the story's tools, and the contents.

Under the title page:

- **Outline**, **Analysis**, **Timeline** and **Glossary** -- the story's
  tools, each with its own page of help: [the outline](/help/writing-a-chapter),
  [analysis and the timeline](/help/targets-and-analysis),
  [the glossary](/help/story-glossary). The author also sees **Edit details**
  and **Archive story** here.
- **The story in numbers** -- a fold with its counts (words, chapters, notes, when it was last written) and, if there is one, the bar towards its word target.
- **Contents** -- every chapter in order, grouped into arcs if the story
  has them. The small initials under a chapter are the people who have
  read it; a red count is notes still pending on it.
- **The whole story, in one file** -- the entire story as a PDF, EPUB,
  Word, Markdown or plain text file, laid out as a manuscript or as a book.
- **Who can write in this story** and the **story dictionary** -- the
  coauthors, and the invented words the spellchecker should leave alone.

## A chapter

The chapter page is the page the site is for.

- Above the title: the way back to the story, and **Previous** and
  **Next** chapter.
- The title, with the chapter's place in the story above it (*Chapter 1
  of 2*), then who wrote it, how long it is and when.
- One row of controls: **Read** or **Review** (**Revise**, on your own
  chapter), **Aa** for how the text
  looks, **Edit** if it is yours, and **More** for downloads and archiving.
- The text, and -- in Review -- the notes beside it.
- At the end, the next chapter in large type, and **All chapters**.

All of it is explained in [reading and reviewing](/help/reading-and-reviewing).

## The top bar

On the left, the three places: **Stories** (the front page), **Wiki**
(the offline copy of the shared-universe wiki) and **Help** (these pages).
On the right, **Search**, which looks through everything -- stories,
chapters, the wiki and every story's glossary -- and **+ Write**, which starts a
new story. Your name opens a small menu: **Your page**, **Account**,
**Tags** (the codes stories carry), **Admin** if you are one, and **Log
out**. The moon switches between light and dark. On a phone all of it is
behind **Menu**.

On a phone the links fold into **Menu**.

## On a phone, as an app

The site can sit on a phone's home screen like an app, with its own icon
and no browser bar round it. On an iPhone, open it in Safari, press
**Share** and **Add to Home Screen**; on Android, Chrome offers **Install
app** (or **Add to Home screen**) from its menu.

Installed or not, the chapters you have opened recently on that device can
be read with no connection -- on a train, in a tunnel. Anything that needs
the server (a note, a draft, the front page) waits for the connection to
come back. Signing out clears the chapters kept on the device.
